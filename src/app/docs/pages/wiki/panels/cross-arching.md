This panel allows you to define the cross arching on the top and back plates. Traditionally, the long arch defines the peak of the arch in the middle of the instrument. At every station, cross arches rise from the edge to meet it. Cross arches therefore are bound by the height of the long arch, and the width of the instrument at their particular "station."

## Stations
By sliding the station height bar you can adjust which section of the cross arch you view on the canvas. The cross section is rendered below the main body of the instrument, and a small dashed line will move up and down the canvas to represent your cross sectional position. 

The cross arching on historical instruments rarely follows the same curve pattern all long the body. In order to accurately represent this, CremonaCad allows to you set the cross arching differently at different stations (heights) of the instrument. Click the 'Set Station at' to set unique curves at your selected height. CremonaCad will do its best to smoothly join these defined sections.

Once you select your first station, you will have to set another before you can define its curve. Station curves must be of the same type (cyloids with cycloids, splines with splines).

## Curated Cycloids
![A curtate cycloid: the path of a point inside a rotating circle.](wiki/cycloid.svg)
Along with catenary curves and splines, which are detailed more in the [long-arching] panel, the cross arching panel allows you to draw curated cycloids. This is another lively topic within the making community, and there doesn't seem to be any direct evidence Cremonese makers used this shape for designing their arching. That said, it tracks some instruments quite well, and would have been a pattern of curves known at the time. 

![A curtate cycloid drawn against a circle.](wiki/cycloidCircle.svg)
One nice quality about cycloids is that they can approximate arc segments of circles, while at the same time "flattening out" at their ends. Many luthiers will describe the "radius" of a cross arching at particular stations, cycloids draw these radii very naturally. 

## Contours and Wireframes
By clicking the wireframe or contour button, you can render a 3D surface of your top and bottom plates. You can rotate this system by dragging on the rendered area with your mouse.
