This panel defines the positions of the corners as well as the arcs that join them to the upper and lower bouts.

The corner position is set using an XY point. This represents the "inner" corner of the rib line. When tracing an instrument, this means it will be inside the plate rather than on its edge. Two arcs are then drawn to shape the corner and the bout. 

## Compounding
Simple instruments may feature a single arc for the corner, and this can look quite good. But many Cremonese makers used more complicated compoinding arcs to draw their corners. Usually the arcs sizes  would get progressively smaller as they reached the corner point. The small arcs at the very end flare more and create a dramatic corner. 

### Straight Sections
Some Cremonese instruments feature a straight section which joined the corner arcs to the bout arcs. This greatly simplified construction of the system during the days of hand making, as a corner cirlce and bout circle could be drawn, and then a tangent line could be found which smoothly joined them. 

To simulate this, simply comound the corner, and set the primary arc radii to a very large value, which approximates a straight line. 

## Viol Corners
Many violas and double basses do not feature small arcs which flare out their corners. Rather they simply smoothly join the bout to a corner point using a single arc. You may toggle this simply geometry on and off.
